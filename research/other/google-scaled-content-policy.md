# Google's Policy on Scaled Content Abuse

**Source:** https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies#scaled-content
**Summarized:** June 2026

---

## What Google considers "Scaled Content Abuse"

Google defines scaled content abuse as generating large amounts of unoriginal content that provides little or no value to users, regardless of how that content is created — whether through automation, human effort, or a combination of both including AI tools.

## Key points

1. **Method doesn't matter.** Google explicitly states the policy applies whether content is generated by AI, humans, or any combination. The issue is the output quality, not the production method.

2. **What gets flagged:**
   - Pages created primarily to manipulate search rankings rather than help users
   - Large-scale content that stitches together content from different sources without adding value
   - Content created across many pages where the content makes little or no sense to users but contains search keywords
   - Mass-produced pages where each page is nearly identical with minor keyword swaps
   - AI-generated content published at scale with no meaningful human review, editing, or curation

3. **What's allowed:**
   - Using AI or automation as a tool in content creation is NOT automatically spam
   - The key test: does the content serve a genuine user purpose, or was it created primarily to manipulate rankings?
   - AI-assisted content with genuine human oversight, editing, and value-add is acceptable

4. **Enforcement:**
   - Sites violating this policy may be demoted or removed from Google Search results entirely
   - Google's March 2024 core update and subsequent updates specifically targeted scaled AI content abuse
   - Recovery requires removing the offending content and submitting a reconsideration request

## Why this matters for AI-SEO content production

This policy is the single most important constraint on any AI content pipeline. Every practitioner in this research (Ryan Law, Lily Ray, Matt Diggity) designs their workflows around this boundary. The consensus: AI drafting is fine, but publishing without human oversight at scale will get penalized.

## Related updates

- **March 2024 Core Update:** First major update explicitly targeting AI-generated spam at scale
- **January 2026 Update:** Lily Ray documented a wave hitting sites with thousands of AI-generated listicles and comparison pages
- **Ongoing:** Google continues to refine detection of low-value AI content across updates
