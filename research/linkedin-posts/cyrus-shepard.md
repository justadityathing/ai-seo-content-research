Do you see *branded* click-through rates (CTRs) decreasing from Google? 

Can publishers do anything about it?

That’s the story I keep hearing from large publishers, based on internal SEO data showing a decrease in brand clicks over the past year.

We call brand search a "moat" in SEO because when people search for you specifically on Google, they are most likely to click through. But what if that effect is weakening? 

The latest research piece by Kevin Indig and Eric Van Buskirk provides much-needed data to explain why this could be happening

https://lnkd.in/gNnmJMfA

Based on their analysis of 846,000 sessions, users spend far, far more time on *Google itself* when AI Overviews are present for branded/navigation searches

In the past, we could rely on branded search to reliably drive clicks. Now, users are much more likely to read + evaluate info on Google itself before clicking. 

Three conclusions for publishers I take away from this piece:

1) Once again, the clear winner is Google - users can now get far more information about you from Google itself without needing to click through
2) Publishers should continue to expect decreased CTRs from branded search as AI Overviews/AI Mode continue to evolve
3) If users are spending more time reading AI Answers about you on Google, it's increasingly critical to make sure that AI Overviews contain accurate + compelling information about your brand

Let's talk Schema and AI visibility, since it's a hot topic these days

A much-discussed Ahrefs study found no improvement in AI citations after Schema appeared in the HTML ⛔ 

It was a very solid study.

But that's not the end of the story...

I looked at 40+ AI studies + experiments to see what they had to say about Schema:

1)  A case study by OtterlyAI across 2000+ URLs showed Google AI Overviews citations increased 1500% after Schema was added. 📈 ChatGPT citations actually dropped. 📉 

2) An AirOps / Kevin Indig study showed pages with JSON-LD had a 6.5% gain over non-JSON-LD pages 📈

3) A 2025 research paper from UC Berkeley showed a very strong correlation between structured data and AI citations, showing a +39% citation lift 📈

4) An analysis of 1000 Google AI Overviews by Digital Applied found "Schema-marked-up pages cited 2.3x more often than unstructured equivalents." 📈

Honestly, it's hard to understand why this is controversial. Structured data helps with Google's Rich Results, and it's generally considered helpful - though there's no reason to overdo it. 

So use Schema for Pete's sake. It might help. 

But don't bank your strategy on it.

How to leverage "lastLongestClicks" for better Google rankings

Google uses many user-interaction signals in its ranking algorithms, as we've learned through patents, trial evidence, and API leaks. 

https://lnkd.in/gH3Sy_zD

Google measures what search results users click on, how long they stay on a webpage, and perhaps most importantly, what page led to a satisfying answer (by ending the search journey).

lastLongestClicks, from the API docs, appears to be one of those important measurements. 

- It's the page that finally gives users their answer.
- It's the page that gives information nobody else is talking about.
- It's the page that users seek out again and again.
- It's the page users trust. 

The biggest shift is to stop thinking about the keyword and solve for the user journey.

A person Googling "movie showtimes" wants more than movie showtimes. They want reviews, cast information, theater information, tickets, and ultimately, to buy a movie ticket.

Your job is to answer the keyword while also finding out what the user really wants.

If they have to go back to Google to complete this journey, you've lost.

The ideal page:

1) Shows the user they are in the right place
2) Answer the main keyword completely
3) Answers obvious and non-obvious follow-up questions
4) Is better than every other top result in at least one or two undeniable ways
5) Saves the user from clicking back to Google by moving the user further along their ultimate journey

Shift your thinking. Happy optimizing.
